## Engine cutaway scene

Original six-cylinder single-overhead-cam cutaway with three rockers per cylinder (intake, exhaust and dedicated engine brake), paired valve bridges, a common rocker shaft, and 24 valves (two intake and two exhaust per cylinder), an exposed camshaft with three lobes per cylinder and roller followers, springs, sectioned block and liners, diesel bowl pistons, connecting rods, crankshaft, and a 2:1 timing drive. Slider-crank motion uses fixed connecting-rod length and a 720-degree valve cycle. The dedicated brake rocker has a collapsible hydraulic slave tip that transmits the brake cam motion only when enabled and briefly opens one exhaust valve near compression top dead center when braking is enabled; fuel is cut and the discharge is illustrated with air particles. The phase indicator follows cylinder 6. The default view focuses on the exposed valvetrain; the engine-block control restores the piston/crank/block cutaway. Site colors stay unchanged. This is illustrative geometry, not production Cummins/Jacobs CAD or a dimensioned model of a particular engine. ## Mechanism-based SOHC study and typography

The animated model follows a generic inline-six heavy-duty SOHC architecture with two valve bridges, roller rocker levers and a lost-motion brake tip. Cam lobes are generated from the inverse roller-envelope pitch curve, using the same smooth lift law as the rocker and valve updates. Springs terminate at their moving retainers. Wrist pins coincide with the slider-crank joint, and the piston skirt and tapered connecting rods have been revised. A pitch-matched 32/48/48/48/64-tooth spur gear train maintains a 2:1 crank-to-cam speed ratio and matched tooth phase. Firing order is 1–5–3–6–2–4. Engine braking includes a small pre-charge and a compression-release event on one exhaust valve, with fuel cut.
